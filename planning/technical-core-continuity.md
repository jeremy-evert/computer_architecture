## Metric maturation

The course deliberately attacks its own early shortcuts:

- **Week 5:** price/capacity, budget share, core/VRAM-style naive proxies.
- **Week 8:** latency versus throughput; dependency makes headline clock insufficient.
- **Week 10:** capacity is separated from latency, bandwidth and locality.
- **Week 12:** core count is separated from speedup, efficiency, synchronization and communication shape.
- **Week 13:** vector/GPU/accelerator labels are separated from measured benefit, setup and data movement.
- **Week 14:** the student chooses the smallest set of metrics that actually bears on the workload decision.

## Evidence-boundary continuity

- Week 5 physical/observable machine and Week 6 RISC-V teaching ISA remain explicitly different contexts.
- Week 7 teaching datapath is not promoted into a claim about a modern physical CPU.
- Week 8 native dependency timing is not turned into an invented exact CPI/pipeline-depth measurement.
- Week 10 memory cliffs are interpreted as regions unless corroborating evidence supports stronger cache-boundary claims.
- Week 11 `/proc` mappings are virtual-mapping evidence, not a physical-frame decoder.
- Week 12 communication delay is a user-space waiting model, not Internet latency.
- Week 13 compiler vectorization evidence is separated from runtime benefit.
- Week 14 separates market drift from learning-driven redesign.

## Semester boundary

Week 14 is the technical hard stop. The Dossier freezes there. Week 15 may curate, Week 16 may echo Architecture during shared Farkle/ML, and Week 17 may reflect from frozen evidence. No Checkpoint 4 exists.
