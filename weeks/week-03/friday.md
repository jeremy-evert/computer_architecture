# Friday - What did the environment actually control?

This is a **graded discussion**, not a solo write-up. The reproducibility
claim you make is only interesting once someone else's environment can be
compared against it.

## Your post

Use Wednesday's two-run receipt (and, if you did the optional container
run, that receipt too) to make one bounded reproducibility claim.

Answer:

1. Which parts of the experiment were fixed by the course source/wrapper?
2. Which facts describe the environment that actually executed the command?
3. Which facts could still differ on another computer?
4. What evidence would another person need to decide whether a difference is meaningful?
5. What would you change in your original reproduction plan?

A strong conclusion does **not** say "the results are identical, therefore the machines are identical."

A stronger shape is:

> The same course source and command produced the same required evidence shape across repeated runs. The receipt records the executing environment, while host hardware and timing may still differ. Another person can reproduce the reasoning task by preserving ____ and recording ____.

## Respond to at least two classmates

For at least two other students' posts:

1. Find one meaningful environment difference between your receipts and
   theirs, and explain whether it actually matters for the experiment or is
   irrelevant noise.
2. Test their bounded reproducibility claim: name the evidence that
   supports it, and name one limitation that should stay attached to the
   claim even if you agree with it.

Do not simply agree. A response that only says "same here" or "nice work"
does not meet the bar.
