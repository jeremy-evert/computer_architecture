# Computer Architecture professional pathway

**Status:** ACCEPTED course doctrine for Fall 2026 and future course builds unless explicitly revised.

## Purpose

Computer Architecture sits close enough to the end of the degree that its professional pathway should help students leave the course able to **show another person what they know and what they can do**.

This is not resume-polish theater. It is a professional-evidence practice.

The governing reasoning loop is:

> **Claim -> Evidence -> Gap -> Decision**

A student makes or inherits a professional claim, identifies evidence that supports it, distinguishes what is genuinely missing from what is merely invisible, and makes a justified next decision.

## Bring work forward; do not restart it

Students should reuse and improve artifacts they already have rather than repeatedly rebuilding generic career materials for each course.

Useful starting artifacts may include:

- a resume;
- GitHub or another code/project portfolio;
- LinkedIn or another professional profile;
- a degree or certification plan;
- target-role research;
- prior course projects;
- presentations, technical explanations, decision logs, or analysis artifacts;
- recommendations or other credible third-party evidence.

An existing artifact is a baseline, not a shortcut. The course asks what the student can now improve, substantiate, or rethink.

## What counts as professional evidence

Professional evidence is broader than a polished profile page. Valid evidence can include:

- a well-documented repository;
- a Machine Dossier checkpoint or excerpt;
- a reproducible experiment;
- an Explain / Defend receipt;
- a technical presentation or explanation;
- a visualization tied to a defensible claim;
- a project decision log;
- a controlled before/after comparison;
- a meaningful contribution to a team;
- a certification, credential, or completed training path;
- a resume or portfolio entry that points to inspectable work.

The strongest evidence is inspectable and specific enough that another person can understand what the student actually did.

## Skill gaps and evidence gaps are different

A **skill gap** means:

> I cannot do this yet, or I cannot do it at the level the target role requires.

An **evidence gap** means:

> I may be able to do this, but I currently cannot show convincing evidence of it to another person.

Those gaps call for different actions. A skill gap may require learning and practice. An evidence gap may require documenting, packaging, demonstrating, or exposing work the student already knows how to do.

Students should learn to name which kind of gap they actually have before deciding what to do next.

## Fall 2026 rhythm

The grading model already reserves two professional-pathway moments:

### Week 1 baseline, ungraded foundation

Use the shared kickoff work to establish or recover the student's current professional baseline. Existing artifacts are welcome.

### Week 14 professional evidence update, 5%

Use the end-of-technical-core perspective to revisit a target role or professional direction.

The student should:

1. state one or more relevant professional claims;
2. identify concrete evidence that supports those claims;
3. compare the evidence against real target-role expectations;
4. identify at least one skill gap and/or evidence gap where justified;
5. improve one or more real artifacts so the evidence becomes stronger or more visible;
6. record what changed and why.

The grade rewards the quality of the reasoning and evidence, not cosmetic polish.

### Week 15 departure package and decision, 5%

Week 15 is a wind-down and professional-pathway week, not a new Architecture-content week.

Students organize the professional evidence they already built, then answer:

> Given the evidence I have now, what is my next professional move, and why?

The next move may involve applying for a role or internship, strengthening a skill, producing missing evidence, finishing a credential, revising a degree plan, expanding a project, or deliberately postponing a choice until better evidence exists.

## Relationship to the Machine Dossier

The Machine Dossier is not a resume assignment, but it can become excellent professional evidence. Students should be encouraged to identify portions of their Architecture work that demonstrate capabilities such as:

- reproducible experimentation;
- systems reasoning;
- performance analysis;
- hardware/software-stack understanding;
- evidence-backed design judgment;
- clear technical communication.

The professional pathway should help students translate real course work into defensible professional claims without distorting the course into career-training busywork.

## Grading doctrine

Professional pathway grading follows these rules:

- reasoning and evidence outrank visual polish;
- expensive tools, premium AI, personal hardware, or paid services cannot raise the attainable grading ceiling;
- students may reuse legitimate prior artifacts and improve them;
- a rough artifact with clear evidence and justified revision can outperform a polished artifact with no reasoning trail;
- students are not penalized for discovering that an earlier career assumption was wrong;
- a justified decision to gather more evidence before acting is a valid professional decision.

The goal is not to force a particular career choice. The goal is to help a student leave Computer Architecture better able to **make a claim, show the evidence, see the gap, and choose the next action**.
