# Prompt 030B — Computer Architecture Weekly Video Teaching Rhythm

Date: 2026-08-24
Owner: Jeremy Evert
Applies to: Prompt 030 Computer Architecture → Optional Computing Commons migration and Prompt 030A three-part grading/consolidation

## Authority

This is a binding owner amendment to Prompts 030 and 030A.

Where an earlier Architecture plan treats shared reflection paperwork, Professional Minds, AI Fluency, multiple micro-assignments, or other wrappers as the mechanism for creating weekly instructor presence, this amendment supersedes that approach.

The online Computer Architecture course should feel like Jeremy is teaching a real class each week, not like students are navigating a pile of assignment objects.

## Weekly teaching rhythm

The normal target is **two or three short Jeremy videos per substantive Architecture week**.

The preferred rhythm is:

### Monday — Teach it

Purpose: teach the Computer Architecture idea for the week.

The Monday video should normally:

- introduce the week's Architecture principle;
- explain why it matters;
- connect it to the week's Architecture Reasoning Odyssey work;
- identify what students should notice, measure, inspect, compare, or be able to explain;
- orient students without turning the video into a second assignment.

This is the primary teaching video for the week.

### Wednesday — Show how Jeremy would do the work

Purpose: model the process of doing the week's work.

The Wednesday video should normally:

- work through the assignment or a close cousin rather than merely reread instructions;
- show Jeremy's reasoning, tool use, mistakes, debugging, measurement choices, and evidence discipline;
- demonstrate what useful evidence looks like;
- show how to recover when a result is confusing or a tool does not behave as expected;
- preserve enough difference from the student's exact task that it remains their work.

This is the online-course equivalent of students watching Jeremy work at the board or projector.

### Friday — Show & Tell / feedback

Purpose: close the weekly loop after student work begins arriving.

The Friday video should normally:

- discuss patterns Jeremy saw in the submitted work;
- highlight strong approaches, clever choices, useful evidence, and recurring mistakes;
- explain what made work convincing or unconvincing;
- discuss bonus-point recognition or opportunities when appropriate;
- correct misunderstandings without creating another required reflection or receipt;
- point forward to the next Architecture idea when useful.

When discussing actual student work, anonymize examples by default unless the student has clearly agreed to be identified.

A Friday video is not required when there is not yet enough submitted work to make it useful. Two good videos are better than a forced third video with no teaching value.

## Grading boundary

These videos are **teaching**, not graded work.

Do not create:

- a video-watching assignment;
- a video reflection;
- a quiz solely to prove viewing;
- a receipt or completion requirement;
- a separate Friday Show & Tell assignment;
- points merely for watching a video.

The existing 15 / 75 / 10 doctrine remains unchanged:

- 15% Weekly Canvas Engagement;
- 75% Architecture Reasoning Odyssey;
- 10% End-of-semester Architecture Reflection.

Watching/using the videos may naturally help the student engage and succeed, but the videos do not create a fourth grading category.

## Canvas/module shape

Flo should structure the active Architecture course so each substantive week can cleanly expose the teaching rhythm without increasing the gradebook object count.

Preferred student-facing module grammar:

1. **Monday — Teach It** — page/media item for the week's teaching video;
2. **Wednesday — How I Would Do It** — page/media item for Jeremy's worked-example/process video;
3. **Architecture Reasoning Odyssey — Week N** — the one substantive graded Architecture assignment for the week when that week has graded Architecture work;
4. **Friday — Show & Tell / Feedback** — page/media item for the post-work feedback video when useful.

The videos/pages may be present as placeholders before the recording is made, but placeholders must be honest and must not pretend a recording exists.

Do not create fake URLs, fake recordings, or generated impersonations of Jeremy.

## Flo's responsibility

Flo owns the structure around the human recording step.

For each substantive week, Flo may:

- create/reconcile the source-level weekly video slots;
- create clear Canvas page/module placeholders where appropriate;
- give each video a week-specific title and purpose;
- connect the Monday and Wednesday video slots to the surviving Architecture Reasoning Odyssey assignment;
- prepare a concise recording outline or bullet checklist from the actual week's Architecture material;
- ensure the Friday slot is positioned after the relevant work rather than before it;
- keep video pages out of the gradebook;
- remove old assignment/reflection clutter that had been serving as a substitute for instructor presence;
- preserve any existing legitimate Architecture teaching media that already fills the slot rather than duplicating it.

Flo may **not** claim a weekly video is complete until a real recording or real media artifact is actually linked/embedded.

## Human gate: recording Jeremy

The act of recording Jeremy is an unavoidable human action and is therefore a legitimate human gate.

However, it is **not** a gate that should stop the Architecture cleanup campaign.

Flo should:

1. fully prepare the course structure and recording slots;
2. record which weeks already have usable teaching media;
3. record which weeks need Monday, Wednesday, and/or Friday recordings from Jeremy;
4. continue the rest of the safe migration/consolidation work;
5. leave Jeremy a compact recording queue rather than repeatedly asking for one video at a time.

Recommended durable recording queue location:

`sidecar/reports/030b_architecture_video_recording_queue.md`

The queue should be week-by-week and show:

- Monday Teach It: existing / needs recording / optional that week;
- Wednesday How I Would Do It: existing / needs recording / optional that week;
- Friday Show & Tell: existing / needs recording / wait for submissions / optional that week;
- the related Reasoning Odyssey assignment;
- a 3–6 bullet recording outline.

## Interaction with the ~17-assignment target

The video rhythm does **not** change Prompt 030A's approximate 17 meaningful gradebook-object target.

Video pages/media items are instructional content, not assignments.

A typical substantive week may therefore contain three or four visible module items while still containing only **one graded Architecture assignment**.

This distinction is intentional: simplify the gradebook without making the online class feel empty.

## Completion test added to the Architecture campaign

Before the Architecture migration is reported complete, verify that:

- the active source/course design documents the Monday / Wednesday / Friday teaching rhythm;
- weekly module/source structure can host two or three videos without creating graded paperwork;
- each surviving weekly Reasoning Odyssey assignment has a clear Monday teaching slot and Wednesday worked-example slot where useful;
- Friday feedback/show-and-tell is structured as teaching content, not a required assignment;
- existing usable Architecture teaching media was reused where appropriate;
- a compact durable recording queue exists for any missing Jeremy recordings;
- missing human recordings are honestly marked as such and do not block safe course cleanup;
- no fake recording, synthetic Jeremy, or invented media link was created.

## Owner intent in one sentence

**Teach it Monday, show how to do it Wednesday, close the loop Friday, and keep the student's required work focused on one real Architecture Reasoning Odyssey assignment.**
