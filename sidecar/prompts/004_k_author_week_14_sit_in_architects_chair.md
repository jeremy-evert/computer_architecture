# Prompt 004_k - Author Week 14: Sit in the Architect's Chair

**Status:** OPEN  
**Depends on:** 004_b through 004_j  
**Checkpoint:** Machine Dossier Checkpoint 3  
**Hard boundary:** Architecture technical instruction ends here

## Why this week exists

The course does not need one more topic. It needs a verdict.

Week 14 returns students to the machine they built in Week 5 and asks whether ten weeks of evidence changed their judgment.

The intellectual test is not “did you replace every part?” A mature answer may be that a Week 5 choice was good, but now the student can defend it for better reasons.

## Central question

> **Given my workload and constraints, what would I build now, and what evidence changed or strengthened my judgment?**

## Non-negotiable role

- no new technical unit;
- no hidden capstone launch;
- no fourth checkpoint;
- no technical dependency deferred to Week 16;
- final Machine Dossier freezes here.

## Monday package

Teach architecture decision-making, not new mechanism content.

Revisit the Week 5 naive metrics and ask which survived:

- clock;
- core count;
- capacity;
- $/GB;
- GPU/spec headline numbers;
- predicted bottleneck.

Students now have richer dimensions available:

- latency;
- throughput;
- locality;
- bandwidth;
- scaling;
- synchronization/communication;
- specialization/data movement;
- cost;
- power/energy where responsibly supported;
- programmability;
- reliability/security where relevant;
- workload fit.

They should choose only the dimensions that matter to their decision.

## Wednesday redesign/defense work

Return to the same or explicitly bounded Week 5 workload/budget.

Refresh time-sensitive product prices/spec evidence.

For several important design choices require:

- original choice;
- current choice;
- changed or deliberately unchanged;
- workload requirement;
- metric/evidence chosen;
- evidence from the dossier;
- tradeoff accepted;
- uncertainty/limitation.

The evidence should come from the student's accumulated course work, not a fresh dump of shopping links.

## Checkpoint 3

Final dossier defense under accepted rubric axes:

- Functions/Runs;
- Concept use;
- Explanation;
- Demonstrability/Reproducibility;
- explicit tradeoff for design decisions.

004_k must decide the exact point/rubric shape within the existing category weight. Do not create new weights.

## Friday Explain / Defend / finale

The final student explanation should feel like an architecture review, not an assignment postmortem.

Require a concise executive defense of the machine/design plus references into the dossier evidence.

## AI Fluency Lens 14

Automate.

Students may automate:

- evidence collection;
- plotting;
- source gathering;
- table generation;
- report assembly;
- consistency checks.

They may not outsource the final judgment.
